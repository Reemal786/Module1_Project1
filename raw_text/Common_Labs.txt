Complete Blood  
Count (CBC)
CBC includes components of white 
blood cells (infection fighting cells), 
red blood cells (oxygen-carrying cells), 
and measurements of how effectively 
the blood is able to clot or respond to 
real or perceived infection.
White Blood Cell 
Count (WBC)
5,000-10,000 WBCs per mm3 or  
5.0-10.0 x 109 WBC per Liter
–  High: Infection, inflammation; may be 
elevated with steroid use
–  Low: Not commonly low
Red Blood Cell 
Count (RBC)
–  Children: 3.8–6.0 million RBCs per 
mcL or 3.8–6.0 x 1012/L 
–  Adults: 4.5–5.5 million RBCs per 
microliter (mcL) or 4.5–5.5 x 1012/
liter (L)
–  High: Dehydration
–  Low: Anemia
Hemoglobin 
(HGB)
–  Children: 9.5–20.5 g/dL or 95–205 
g/L 
–  Adults: 14–17.4 grams per 
deciliter (g/dL) or 140–174 grams 
per liter (g/L)
–  Low: Anemia
Hematocrit (HCT) –  Children: 29%–59% or 0.29–0.59 
volume fraction  
–  Adults: 42%–52% or 0.42–0.52 
volume fraction 
–  Low: Anemia
Mean 
Corpuscular 
Volume (MCV)
– 6 months-23 months: 69.5-81.7 fL
–  24 months-35 months: 71.3-84.0 
fL
– 3-5 years: 77.2-89.5 fL
– 6-11 years: 77.8-91.1 fL
– 12-14 years: 79.9-93.0 fL
– 15-17 years: 82.5-98.0 fL
– Adults: 78.2-97.9 fL
Measures the size of the average red 
blood cell.  
–  Low: Microcytic anemia
–  High: Macrocytic anemia
Mean 
Corpuscular 
Hemoglobin 
(MCH)
27-33 pg Measures the average amount of 
hemoglobin in a single red blood cell.
–  Low: Iron deficiency anemia
–  High: Can indicate macrocytic anemia
The following are labs that are commonly drawn in neuromuscular centers.  
Normal ranges have been provided by mayocliniclabs.com. Common reasons 
for obtaining these labs, as well as possible reasons for high and low values, 
are included. This list is not exhaustive and any abnormal values, questions, or 
concerns should be discussed with your neuromuscular center.

Renal Panel Evaluates the health and function  
of the kidneys.
Albumin 3.5 – 5.0 g/dL Protein filtered by the kidneys.
–  High: May indicate dehydration
–  Low: Usually occurs with edema
Anion Gap  3.5-5.0 
–  Over 7 years: 7-15

–  High: May indicate acidosis

–  4-5 years: 19-27 mmol/L
–  6-7 years: 20-28 mmol/L

imbalance.  Bicarbonate levels are

calcium can cause heart arrhythmias
–  Low: Poorly functioning parathyroid 
glands or a lack of vitamin D synthesis 
of the body; may indicate kidney failure

Notes
Carbon Dioxide 23-29 mEq/L Collected and evaluated with bicarbonate. 
An imbalance indicates an electrolyte 
imbalance. 
–  High: In Duchenne, can indicate 
respiratory acidosis, vomiting
–  Low: In Duchenne, can indicate 
respiratory alkalosis, diarrhea
Creatinine –  0-11 months: 0.17-0.42 mg/dL
–  1-5 years: 0.19-0.49 mg/dL
–  6-10 years: 0.26-0.61 mg/dL
–  11-14 years: 0.35-0.86 mg/dL
–  > or =15 years: 0.74-1.35 mg/dL
Indicates muscle breakdown.
–  High: May indicate kidney dysfunction, 
dehydration, low blood volume
–  Low: Uncommon
Chloride 98-107 mmol/L Indication of fluid/electrolyte balance in 
the body.
–  High: May indicate dehydration, kidney 
dysfunction, diabetes
–  Low: May indicate over-hydration, 
prolonged vomiting
Glucose 70-140 mg/dL Screen for diabetes.
–  High: May indicate poor glucose 
regulation by insulin in the body; a 
glucose tolerance test will be needed 
for further evaluation
Phosphorous –  1-4 years: 4.3-5.4 mg/dL
–  5-13 years: 3.7-5.4 mg/dL
–  14-15 years: 3.5-5.3 mg/dL
–  16-17 years: 3.1-4.7 mg/dL
–  > or =18 years: 2.5-4.5 mg/dL
Screen for bone, parathyroid and kidney 
disease.
–  High: May indicate kidney dysfunction
–  Low: Phosphate shifts in the body (fluid/
electrolyte imbalances)
Potassium 3.6-5.2 mmol/L Indication of fluid/electrolyte balance in 
the body.
–  High: Can be dangerous and should be 
monitored closely
–  Low: Common with vomiting, diarrhea, 
iron deficiency
Sodium 135-145 mmol/L Indication of fluid/electrolyte balance in 
the body.
–  High: May indicate excessive loss of 
body fluid
–  Low: Vomiting, diarrhea, use of some 
diuretics, metabolic acidosis
Blood Urea 
Nitrogen (BUN)
–  <12 months: not established
–  1-17 years: 7-20 mg/dL
–  > or =18 years: 8-24 mg/dL
Screens for kidney function.
–  High: Kidney issues or causes that may 
lead to kidney issues
–  Low: Uncommon but may indicate 
severe starvation, severe liver disease

Endocrine Labs
25 
Hydroxyvitamin D 
(25 OH Vit D)
–  <16 years: 24-86 pg/mL
–  > or =16 years: 18-64 pg/mL
–  Low: A lack of vitamin D stores in the 
body
Total Coenzyme 
Q 10 (CoQ10) 
Profile 
–  <18 years: 320-1,558 mcg/L
–  > or =18 years: 433-1,532 mcg/L
CoQ10 is essential for mitochondrial 
activity. A low value may reflect a need 
for supplemental CoQ10.
Parathyroid 
Hormone (PTH)
–  4 weeks-11 months: 8.0-61 pg/mL
–  12 months-10 years: 11-59 pg/mL
–  11 years-17 years: 15-68 pg/mL
–  18 years and older: 15-65 pg/mL
Serum calcium levels stimulate PTH 
release. The values are interpreted  
with calcium and phosphorous levels.  
A high level usually reflects a vitamin D 
deficiency.
Insulin (Free and 
Total)
– Free Insulin: 2.6-24.9 mcIU/mL
– Total Insulin: 2.6-24.9 mcIU/mL
Insulin regulates the utilization of 
glucose by the body. High insulin may 
indicate early type II diabetes (insulin 
resistance); low levels indicate type I 
(insulin dependent) diabetes.
Glucose 
(Random, Non-
Fasting)
–  0-11 months: not established
–  > or =1 year: 70-140 mg/dL
A random glucose >200 mg/dL may 
indicate the presence of diabetes.
Creatine Kinase 
(CK)
–  < or =3 months: not established
–  >3 months: 39-308 U/L
CK will always be elevated in Duchenne, 
reflecting skeletal muscle injury.
Gamma-Glutamyl 
Transferase Test 
(GGT)
–  0-11 months: <178 U/L
–  12 months-6 years: <21 U/L
–  7-12 years: <24 U/L
–  13-17 years: <43 U/L
–  > or =18 years: 8-61 U/L
–  High: May indicate liver disease; 
often interpreted with the alkaline 
phosphatase (ALP) level. Normal 
GGT with high ALP is consistent with 
skeletal disease.
Hemoglobin A1C 
(HgbA1C)
Over 18:
–  4.0 – 5.6% normal
–  5.7 - 6.4% Pre-diabetic; increased 
risk for Diabetes
–  >6.5% - diabetes
Result indicates potential risk for the 
development of pre-diabetes/diabetes.
